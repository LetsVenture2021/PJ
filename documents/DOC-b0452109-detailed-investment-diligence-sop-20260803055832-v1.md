## Objective

Define a deeply detailed, governance-first operating procedure for end-to-end investment due diligence so every opportunity is assessed with evidence traceability, risk transparency, and decision-grade consistency before committee review.

## Scope

Applies to all pre-investment opportunities from intake through committee submission and archival. Covers strategic fit assessment, market and customer validation, product diligence, team evaluation, financial model stress testing, legal and compliance checks, risk register management, recommendation drafting, cross-functional approvals, publication controls, and audit retention. Includes normal-track and expedited-track execution, while preserving mandatory control gates.

## Roles

Deal Lead (accountable owner): owns diligence plan, gate decisions, and final recommendation framing. Analyst (primary operator): builds evidence library, validates factual claims, updates risk register, and drafts memo sections. Finance Reviewer (control owner): verifies assumptions, scenario analysis, unit economics, and valuation logic. Legal/Compliance Reviewer (control owner): validates entity records, legal exposures, regulatory considerations, and required disclosures. Operations Coordinator (process custodian): tracks milestones, enforces completeness checks, and controls version integrity. Committee Sponsor (approver): confirms decision readiness and strategic alignment.

## Prerequisites

Opportunity record and identifier must exist in the pipeline system; minimum source pack must be received (deck, cap table snapshot, financial statements or model, incorporation details); diligence workspace must be initialized with controlled naming conventions; evidence index must be created with source provenance fields; reviewer assignments and service-level dates must be agreed; conflict-of-interest declarations must be captured; risk taxonomy and scoring scale must be loaded for this opportunity.

## Procedure

Step 1 - Intake and qualification: verify baseline document completeness, evaluate mandate fit, and classify diligence depth level. Step 2 - Diligence plan definition: map hypotheses, information gaps, owners, deadlines, and required external checks. Step 3 - Evidence buildout: gather market sizing inputs, competitor comparisons, customer voice evidence, product reliability signals, and operating metrics; record provenance for each claim. Step 4 - Financial and scenario review: normalize model assumptions, test sensitivity ranges, evaluate runway and dilution impacts, and compare valuation against comparable transactions. Step 5 - Legal and compliance review: assess corporate structure, governance posture, material contractual obligations, and regulatory constraints. Step 6 - Risk register synthesis: score probability and impact for each risk, assign mitigations, and define trigger thresholds and escalation owners. Step 7 - Recommendation development: produce thesis, anti-thesis, assumptions register, and decision options with explicit tradeoffs. Step 8 - Cross-functional challenge session: run structured review, capture objections, resolve factual conflicts, and document residual uncertainties. Step 9 - Approval and freeze: obtain sponsor confirmation, finalize the governed source document, seal quality metadata, and lock revision lineage. Step 10 - Distribution and archive: export audience-ready PDF, issue through approved channel, and archive all artifacts and evidence references for post-decision retrieval.

## Exceptions

Expedited processing is permitted only when sponsor-authorized and may shorten cycle times, but cannot skip legal/compliance review, risk register completion, or evidence-backed recommendation requirements. Missing critical evidence creates a hold state until resolved or explicitly waived by accountable approver. Conflicting high-impact findings trigger mandatory escalation and adjudication before final submission.
